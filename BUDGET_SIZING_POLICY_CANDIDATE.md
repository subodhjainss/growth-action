# Candidate budget-sizing policy

Status: UNVALIDATED, not executable. FR-6 and AT-10 preparation. Uses DECISION_ENGINE_DESIGN.md and the existing safety/evidence architecture. No final numeric thresholds, new models, Meta writes or V1 scope expansion.

## Historical analysis completed privately

Examined budget activity against daily sales-ad-set evidence, omitting the specifically provisional latest date. Used three-day nonoverlapping pre/post windows excluding the intervention day as an exploratory diagnostic, not a validated observation period. Flagged incomplete comparisons and adjacent budget changes. Even an unflagged pair can contain other interventions, changing objectives/control, promotions or attribution revisions. Repeated objects and overlapping windows are not independent experiments.

The sample contains sparse usable comparisons and heavily clustered changes in some test objects. It supports inspectable candidate selection, not causal effects, calibrated safety, an optimal increment or a validated response curve. Real counts, values and per-case conclusions remain private.

## Candidate policy to test

1. Establish action eligibility first. No amount is selected to rescue a blocked action. Identify current controlling object, budget type, status, measurement validity and unresolved interventions.
2. Distinguish business goal, minimum, aggregate ambition and any confirmed object/spend-specific expectation. Record whether overall growth is permitted. That permission is not a financial limit or incremental-return estimate.
3. Build possible next-step amounts from this object's recorded interventions where available. Other comparable objects can supply labelled research candidates, but similarity and coverage must be justified. Historical popularity is not evidence of correctness. Never set a universal median step.
4. Exclude candidates that breach explicitly recorded account limits, absolute/percentage bounds or permitted financial exposure. Limits must have a source and version. Unknown limits remain unknown; do not invent them. Daily budget comparison is not a hard cap on actual spend.
5. For a supported expansion candidate, test one small nonzero admissible step anchored to relevant history. Selection favors limiting unresolved exposure; no claim that the smallest step maximizes revenue. If evidence cannot distinguish candidates, preserve that uncertainty rather than returning a spurious precise optimum.
6. For reduction, do not mirror the expansion percentage automatically. Examine persistent unacceptable economics, the current budget and recent reductions before proposing an admissible cut. Returning to an observed earlier level is a candidate only if relevant context is comparable; earlier good ROAS does not prove a lower budget will restore it.
7. Make confidence affect whether the amount is actionable, rather than hiding an invented confidence-to-percentage formula. A blocked candidate retains magnitude unset; conditional amounts remain behind Why for operator-test preparation.
8. Record what the next import must establish: actual implementation, dates observed after it, compatible attribution maturity, spend and purchase/value evidence, changed delivery and relevant contradictory signals. Review/stop conditions need validation. No automatic enforcement or universal waiting time.

## Freeze and comparison

Record candidate action, next amount, calculation/evidence references, policy version, missing limits and counterarguments before the operator's answer. Compare the justification and downside, not just matching values. If we revise after reveal, test on separate cases before encoding final parameters.

Useful checks include: does the amount address a real supported action; could it exceed permitted exposure; does it stack an unobserved edit; does reasoning confuse average with incremental return; is the explanation understandable; and is refusal to choose justified rather than generic uncertainty?

## What this still does not solve

No optimal-budget estimator, causal test or forecast. No established confidence cutoffs, universally sufficient sample, calibrated wait period or final scale/reduce percentage. The first operator comparison validates candidate usefulness and safety, not statistically superior performance. Outcome observations strengthen later evaluation but do not automatically rewrite policy. Controlled improvement remains V1.1.

## Sensitivity check

The private review also repeats eligibility with seven-day pre/post comparisons. Fewer isolated observations remain. Divergent outcomes after reductions reinforce that historic step size and outcome cannot be adopted as a universal policy. These are analysis windows, not product observation timers.
